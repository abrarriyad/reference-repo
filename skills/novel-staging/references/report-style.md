# Design conventions for the staging report

The report is generated by `scripts/novel-staging.mjs render --html`, with the styles and page script all inlined in `renderHtml()`. **Change styling in that one place only.**

It shares one visual language with the other reports: cool-grey stamp paper + rust-red seal marks, 1600 wide, zero external dependencies, opens offline with a double-click. What differs from them is that **this report is adjustable**: one top-down plan per scene, people can be dragged, and exporting afterwards gives a new staging.json.

## Structure

```
Header    Title · opening blocking · change status · quality-gate badge · (only when changed) Revert · Export JSON
KPI band  Scenes (how many have someone absent at the opening) / locations (how many recognized from images) / people present at the opening / extras
(Problem banner)  Appears only when a quality gate has not passed
01 Opening blocking by scene   One large card per scene (the body of the report)
02 Locations and recognized items   One card per location: scene image + item list
03 Quality gates   The ✓/✗ checklist as of render time
```

## One card per scene

On the left is the **top-down plan** (16:10, north up and south down, west left and east right, with compass letters in a ring outside the plan); on the right, from top to bottom: this location's scene image (small) → the selected person → extras → the opening blocking sentence (with a copy button) → why it was planned this way → this scene's live checks.

- **Items** are drawn as boxes per `plan`; boxes that can be sat or stood on are rust-coloured; round ones are drawn as circles. The name is attached to the box's upper-left corner without wrapping: when a person stands in the middle of an item, the name is not covered.
- **People** are dots with the first character of the name written on the dot and the full name below; a seated person's colour is lighter; a small triangle points the way they face. The dot's position is computed from "item + which side + how far along" (`placePoints`), and so is always one and the same thing as the blocking sentence.
- **Dragging**: hold a person and drag elsewhere on the plan; on release they land on the nearest side of the nearest item (`nearestPlace`); released inside a sittable item, they sit on it. While dragging, the target item lights up, and the box of the same item on the scene image at the right lights up too. When the item or the side changes, the person automatically turns to face it; a person dragged in from the tray is standing, and sits down on landing on a sittable item (`moveTo`).
- **The tray** is below the plan: people not present at the opening. Dragging into the plan means present, and dragging back into the tray means not present at the opening.
- **Click** a person: on the right appear sitting / standing, facing (a compass of eight directions) and remove.
- **Extras**: add/remove buttons + a line of text for "where"; when the count goes to 0, "where" is cleared automatically.
- **Live checks**: after every change this scene is recomputed by the same rules as `validate` (`sceneProblems`), with problems listed in rust-red.

The algorithm on the page and the command line are one and the same copy: the whole of `stagingCore()` is embedded in the page with `toString()`. So the function body of `stagingCore` must not reference anything outside it, and the self-test rebuilds a copy separately from its source to check it.

## Changes, saving, export

- Every change is stored in this computer's browser (`localStorage`, keyed by file content) and survives a refresh; once the file has been re-rendered and its content has changed, the old changes are no longer applied. In private mode it cannot be stored, but editing and exporting still work.
- The header shows "edited on the page, not yet exported", and the export button turns solid rust-red.
- **Export JSON** downloads the edited staging.json, which can be fed back into `validate` / `render` / `blocking` as usual. The same two pitfalls: escape `<` in the embedded data to prevent `</script` from truncating it; delay the `revokeObjectURL` call so Safari does not save an empty file.
- **Revert to the version in the file** requires two clicks (the first turns it into "click again to revert", and a second click within three seconds does the revert), to guard against slips; the browser's confirm dialog is not used.

## Location cards

Two per row. On the scene image (present only if there is an image), each recognized item is drawn per `box`; when the mouse moves to a row in the list or an item on the plan, the corresponding box on the image lights up and shows the name. With no image it is a placeholder stating that the image should be placed at `images/<location name>-sheet.png`. Locations whose `readFrom` is `prompt` are marked "no image, reckoned from the design prompt".

## Quality floor

- All model-generated data goes through `esc()` escaping
- Chinese copy avoids single-character words (write the full words for sitting / standing and due north / due east, not single characters)
- `@media print`: hide buttons, the tray and the selection panel
- `prefers-reduced-motion` turns off animation
